Lesson 1

Identity & Topics

Saying what something is, asking simple questions, and connecting nouns with の.
1

XはYです — X is Y

Stick です on the end of a noun to make a complete sentence: N + です = "it is N." Add a topic with は when you need to specify who/what you're talking about.

PatternXは Yです。 → As for X, it is Y.
学生です。
(I) am a student.
せんこうは日本語です。
My major is Japanese.
わたしはソラ・キムです。
I'm Sora Kim.
Japanese drops subjects all the time. 学生です alone is a perfectly fine sentence — context fills in the "I." Only add X は when you're introducing or switching the topic.
は is written は but pronounced wa when it's the topic particle.
2

Question sentences — Adding か

Slap か on the end of a statement and you have a question. No question mark needed (though casual writing uses one).

Pattern[statement]です + か → question
りゅうがくせいです。
(You're) an international student.
りゅうがくせいですか。
Are you an international student?
なんさいですか。
How old are you?
なんねんせいですか。
What year (in school) are you?
Question words to memorize now: なん/なに (what), なんじ (what time), なんさい (how old), なんねんせい (what year in school), なんばん (what number).
3

NounのNoun — Linking two nouns

の glues two nouns together. The second noun is the main thing; the first one describes or restricts it. Think of it as the all-purpose connector: possessive, modifier, association — same particle.

PatternNoun₁ の Noun₂ → the Noun₁'s Noun₂ / Noun₂ of Noun₁
たけしさんの でんわばんごう
Takeshi's phone number
だいがくの せんせい
a college professor
日本ごの がくせい
a student of Japanese
日本の だいがく
a college in Japan
The Noun₁のNoun₂ chunk acts like one big noun — you can drop it anywhere a single noun fits: たけしさんのおかあさんは こうこうのせんせいです。
Lesson 2

Things, Places, Possession

Pointing at stuff (これ・それ・あれ), location words, and saying things aren't.
1

これ・それ・あれ・どれ — this / that / that over there / which

These are the こそあど demonstratives in their standalone form — they replace a noun entirely ("this one," "that one").

WordUse when…
これThe thing is near you (the speaker)
それThe thing is near your listener
あれThe thing is away from both of you
どれ"Which one?" (three or more choices)
これは いくらですか。
How much is this?
2

この・その・あの・どの + Noun — this/that/which + noun

Same こそあど logic, but these attach to a noun directly. Use these when you need to specify which thing — "this book," not just "this."

Differenceこれ = "this (one)" alone  |  この本 = "this book"
この時計は いくらですか。
How much is this watch?
Never say これ本. Standalone form (これ) ≠ modifier form (この).
3

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ — here / there / over there / where

Same family, but for places. Use them like nouns: with は, が, で, に, etc.

トイレは どこですか。
Where's the bathroom?
ここです。
(It's) here.
4

だれのNoun — Whose ___ ?

To ask whose something is, use だれの + the noun. The の is just the regular linking-noun の from L1.

これは だれのかばんですか。
Whose bag is this?
ソラさんのです。
It's Sora's. (noun omitted — clear from context)
5

Nounも — ___ too / also

も replaces は or が to mean "also/too." One particle, completely swaps in.

PatternX は Yです。 + Z も Yです。
わたしは がくせいです。たけしさんも がくせいです。
I'm a student. Takeshi is also a student.
Don't stack: ×わたしはも. It's ○わたしも.
6

Nounじゃないです — ___ is not ___

Negative of です. Replace です with じゃないです (casual-polite) or じゃありません (more formal).

Affirmative → Negative学生です → 学生 じゃないです / 学生 じゃありません
わたしは 中国人 じゃないです。
I'm not Chinese.
じゃ is a contraction of では. In writing you may see ではありません — same meaning, stiffer.
7

～ね / ～よ — Sentence-ending particles

These tiny particles at the end of a sentence carry social meaning. Pick the right one or you sound a bit off.

ね"…right?" — seeking agreement / confirming shared knowledge
よ"FYI / let me tell you" — sharing info the listener doesn't have
いいてんきですね。
Nice weather, isn't it? (we both see it)
あ、それ ちがいますよ。
Oh, that's wrong, FYI. (you didn't know)
Lesson 3

Verbs & Particles

Verb conjugation groups, present tense, place/time particles, and the topic particle は.
1

Verb groups & ます-form — The three conjugation classes

Every Japanese verb falls into one of three groups. Knowing the group tells you exactly how to conjugate. Genki calls them ru-, u-, and irregular verbs.

GroupPatternExamples
る-verbs
(Group 2)		End in -eる or -iる. Conjugate by chopping the る.たべる, みる, ねる, おきる
う-verbs
(Group 1)		End in any other -u sound. Conjugate by changing the final kana.のむ, かく, よむ, はなす, いく
IrregularJust two of them — memorize.する, くる
Some verbs look like る-verbs but aren't — かえる (to return), はいる (to enter), はしる (to run), きる (to cut) are sneaky う-verbs. You'll learn the list by exposure.
2

Present tense ます-form — Polite present/future

ます-form is the polite verb form. It covers both present habit ("I drink coffee") and future intent ("I'll drink coffee") — context tells you which.

AffirmativeNegative
る-verb
たべる		たべますたべません
う-verb
のむ		のみますのみません
するしますしません
くるきますきません
う-verb ruleChange final -u → -i, then add ます. のむ → のみ + ます
コーヒーを のみます。
I drink coffee. / I'll drink coffee.
テレビを みません。
I don't watch TV.
3

Particles を, で, に, へ — Marking objects, places, times, directions

Particles tag each noun with its job in the sentence. Memorize the role, not just the translation.

ParticleJobExample
をDirect objectコーヒーをのみます
でPlace where action happensカフェでのみます
にSpecific time (3時に) or destination (with come/go)7時におきます
へDirection of movement (≈ に for going somewhere)がっこうへいきます
を is pronounced o, not wo. Only used as a particle — never inside words.
4

Time references with に — When you need に, when you don't

Use に with specific clock times and dates. Don't use it with relative time words.

に requiredNo に
3時に, 月よう日に, 9月にきょう, あした, いま, まいにち, あさ
3時に おきます。
I get up at 3.
あした べんきょうします。
I'll study tomorrow. (no に)
5

Frequency adverbs — How often

These go right before the verb (or wherever — Japanese word order is flexible). When using frequency words, the topic particle は often appears.

よくoften
ときどきsometimes
あまり (+ neg.)not very often
ぜんぜん (+ neg.)not at all
わたしは ぜんぜん さけを のみません。
I don't drink alcohol at all.
あまり and ぜんぜん require a negative verb. ×ぜんぜんのみます.
6

Word order — Surprisingly flexible

As long as particles are right and the verb stays at the end, you can reorder freely. The thing right before the verb tends to get focus.

メアリーさんは あした 京都へ いきます。
Mary is going to Kyoto tomorrow.
あした メアリーさんは 京都へ いきます。
Same meaning, slight emphasis shift.
7

The topic particle は (revisited) — Introducing the frame

は marks the topic — what the sentence is about. It's not a subject marker; it's a "as for ___" marker. The actual subject of the verb may be different.

メアリーさんは、しゅうまつ なにをしますか。
What does Mary do on weekends? (frame = Mary)
ばんごはんは たべません。
I won't eat dinner. (frame = dinner, even though it's the object)
Lesson 4

Past Tense, Existence, Locations

Past forms of です, the existence verbs あります/います, and saying where things are.
1

Xがあります / Xがいます — There is / There are

Two verbs of existence — and which one you pick depends on whether the thing is animate or not.

VerbUse for
ありますInanimate things: objects, plants, places, abstract things
いますAnimate things: people, animals (anything that moves on its own)
つくえの上に 本が あります。
There's a book on the desk.
こうえんに こどもが います。
There are children in the park.
×つくえの上に 本が います — books don't move on their own.
2

Describing where things are — X は [place] にあります

To say "X is at [place]," use X は [place] に あります/います. The topic は marks what you're locating; に marks the location.

Pattern[thing]は [place]に あります／います
メアリーさんは としょかんに います。
Mary is in the library.
ぎんこうは デパートの となりに あります。
The bank is next to the department store.
Location words: 上(on), 下(under), 前(in front), 後ろ(behind), となり(next to), 近く(near), 中(inside). These all combine with の: つくえの上.
3

Past tense of nouns/adjectives — でした and じゃなかったです

To put です into the past, just swap the ending.

AffirmativeNegative
Non-pastがくせいですがくせいじゃないです
Pastがくせいでしたがくせいじゃなかったです
きのうは 月よう日でした。
Yesterday was Monday.
テストは あまり むずかしくなかったです。
The test wasn't very hard.
4

Past tense of verbs — ました and ませんでした

To make a polite verb past, replace ます/ません with ました/ませんでした.

AffirmativeNegative
Non-pastのみますのみません
Pastのみましたのみませんでした
きのう ともだちに あいました。
I met a friend yesterday.
しゅうまつは べんきょうしませんでした。
I didn't study over the weekend.
5

もう and まだ — Already / not yet

Used together as a pair. もう + past affirmative = "already (did)." まだ + present negative (despite the English "yet") = "haven't yet."

もう ひるごはんを たべました。
I already ate lunch.
まだ たべていません。
I haven't eaten yet.
"I haven't eaten yet" is not ×まだたべませんでした. The standard answer uses the ている negative (covered in L7).
6

Counting with counters — Numbers come with classifier words

Japanese doesn't say "two books" — it says "two-classifier books." Different categories of thing get different counters. Memorize a handful and recognize the pattern.

CounterFor
～つ / ～こgeneral small objects
～にん / ～りpeople (ひとり, ふたり, さんにん…)
～さつbooks, magazines
～ほん／ぼん／ぽんlong thin things (pens, bottles)
～まいflat things (paper, tickets)
Lesson 5

Adjectives & Liking

The two adjective classes, past forms, modifying nouns, and the が-quirk with 好き.
1

い-adjectives — Present tense

い-adjectives end in い and conjugate themselves — no need for です to make a complete sentence (though we add it for politeness). The negative drops い and adds くない.

AffirmativeNegative
い-adjたかいですたかくないです
この本は おもしろいです。
This book is interesting.
あの レストランは 高くないです。
That restaurant isn't expensive.
いい (good) is irregular — its negative is よくない, not ×いくない. It actually conjugates off the older form よい.
2

な-adjectives — Present tense

な-adjectives behave like nouns — they need です, and their negative uses じゃないです (same as nouns).

AffirmativeNegative
な-adjしずかですしずかじゃないです
この まちは しずかです。
This town is quiet.
げんきじゃないです。
I'm not feeling well.
3

Past tense for adjectives — い-adj and な-adj behave differently

い-adjectives have their own past form. な-adjectives borrow the past forms of です.

Past affirmativePast negative
い-adjたかかったですたかくなかったです
な-adjしずかでしたしずかじゃなかったです
テストは むずかしかったです。
The test was difficult.
ホテルは しずかじゃなかったです。
The hotel wasn't quiet.
いい past = よかったです. Past negative = よくなかったです. Don't say ×いかった.
4

Adjectives modifying nouns — Different connectors

Put the adjective before the noun. い-adjectives attach directly. な-adjectives need な between them and the noun.

い-adjおもしろい本 — "an interesting book"
な-adjしずかなまち — "a quiet town"
やすい レストランで たべました。
I ate at a cheap restaurant.
きれいな こうえんに いきました。
I went to a beautiful park.
That's why they're called "な-adjectives" — the な only shows up in this attaching-to-noun position.
5

好き(な) / きらい(な) — Liking and disliking — note the が

In Japanese, "liking" works grammatically like "is pleasing." The subject is the liker (marked は); the thing liked is marked with が, not を.

Pattern[liker]は [thing]が 好きです／きらいです
わたしは コーヒーが 好きです。
I like coffee.
ソラさんは なっとうが 大きらいです。
Sora hates natto.
大好き (love) and 大きらい (hate) are stronger than とても好き. Use them.
In English: "I like X" — X is the object. In Japanese: Xが好き — X is the subject of "is liked." Don't use を here.
6

～ましょう / ～ましょうか — Let's ___ / Shall we ___?

Replace ます with ましょう for "let's," or ましょうか for a softer "shall we?"

いっしょに ひるごはんを たべましょう。
Let's eat lunch together.
コーヒーを のみましょうか。
Shall we drink coffee?
Lesson 6

て-form, Permission, Prohibition

The all-purpose te-form, requests, permission, prohibition, and connecting clauses.
1

The て-form — How to form it

The て-form is the most important verb form in Japanese. It's the gateway to requests, permission, ongoing actions, connecting clauses, and dozens of grammar patterns later on. Memorize the rules.

GroupRuleExample
る-verbsDrop る, add てたべる → たべて
する→ してべんきょうする → べんきょうして
くる→ きて
う-verbs: depends on the final kana
〜う・つ・る→ ってかう→かって, まつ→まって, とる→とって
〜む・ぬ・ぶ→ んでのむ→のんで, あそぶ→あそんで
〜く→ いてかく→かいて
〜ぐ→ いでおよぐ→およいで
〜す→ してはなす→はなして
いく is irregular: いって, not ×いいて.
2

～てください — Please do ___

Add ください to the て-form for a polite request/command. Standard request form.

もう一度 言ってください。
Please say it once more.
ここに 名前を 書いてください。
Please write your name here.
3

～てもいいです — It's OK to ___ / You may ___

Permission. て-form + も + いいです.

ここに すわっても いいですか。
May I sit here?
写真を とっても いいです。
You may take pictures.
4

～てはいけません — You must not ___ / Don't ___

Prohibition. て-form + は + いけません. Often softened to ～ちゃだめ in casual speech.

ここで しゃしんを とっては いけません。
You can't take photos here.
じゅぎょうちゅう ねては いけません。
You mustn't sleep during class.
5

Connecting clauses with て-form — "___, and then ___"

Use the て-form like a comma between verbs. It links actions in a loose "and" — usually in sequence, sometimes simultaneously. The tense of the whole sentence comes from the final verb.

あさ おきて、コーヒーを のみました。
I got up and drank coffee.
としょかんへ いって、べんきょうします。
I'll go to the library and study.
Te-form also works for adjectives and nouns: やすくて、おいしい (cheap and tasty), 学生で、20さいです (a student, and 20 years old). Different rules — covered in L7.
6

～から — Because ___

Tack から onto the end of a reason clause. "[Reason] から、 [result]." The reason comes first in Japanese.

あした テストが ありますから、 今日 べんきょうします。
Because I have a test tomorrow, I'll study today.
Order matters. Genki-English puts "because" anywhere, but in Japanese から always sits at the end of the reason clause.
Lesson 7

Progressive & Connecting Clauses

～ている (ongoing actions & resulting states), connecting adjectives, person descriptions.
1

～ています — action in progress — I'm ___ing (right now)

て-form + います = ongoing action. Same shape as English present continuous.

ソラさんは いま ばんごはんを 食べています。
Sora is eating dinner now.
何を していますか。
What are you doing?
2

～ています — resulting state — Verbs of change

This is the trap. With certain verbs (ones describing a momentary change), ～ています means "is in the state of having ___ed" — not "in the middle of ___ing."

Verb～ています means…
けっこんする (get married)けっこんしています = is married (state)
しる (find out)しっています = knows
すむ (move into)東京にすんでいます = lives in Tokyo
もつ (take hold of)もっています = has / is holding
The negative of しっています is しりません ("I don't know") — not ×しっていません. This is the most common L7 mistake.
3

Connecting verb/adjective clauses — te-forms across word classes

Same logic as L6's verb-て, but extended to adjectives and nouns. Use these to chain descriptions: "X is A, and (also) B."

TypeTe-formExample
い-adj (drop い, add くて)やすい → やすくてやすくて、おいしい
な-adj (add で)しずか → しずかでしずかで、きれいです
noun (add で)学生 → 学生で学生で、20さいです
この レストランは やすくて、おいしいです。
This restaurant is cheap and tasty.
メアリーさんは アメリカ人で、ロンドンに すんでいます。
Mary is American, and lives in London.
Irregular: いい → よくて.
4

Verbs of movement + に行く/来る/帰る — Going somewhere to do something

To say "go somewhere to do something," use the verb stem (ます-form minus ます) + に + a go/come/return verb.

Pattern[verb-stem] に いく／くる／かえる
デパートへ かばんを かいに 行きました。
I went to the department store to buy a bag.
日本へ 日本語を べんきょうしに 来ました。
I came to Japan to study Japanese.
する-verbs use the noun directly: かいものに行く (go shopping), べんきょうしに行く.
5

Counting people — ～にん, plus two irregulars

Use ～にん for counting people — except the first two, which are irregular.

1 personひとり
2 peopleふたり
3 peopleさんにん
4 peopleよにん (not よんにん)
7 peopleしちにん or ななにん
Lesson 8

Short Forms — The Plain Form Toolkit

Plain/dictionary forms — the foundation for casual speech, quoting, and tons of later grammar.
1

Short (plain) forms — present — What they are and why they matter

Every verb has a polite form (～ます) and a plain (short / dictionary) form. Plain forms are what you hear between friends, what's used in subordinate clauses (the part before と思う, から, とき...), and what you'll see in books, manga, and most anime dialogue. They're essential.

AffirmativeNegative
verb (dict.)たべる, のむたべない, のまない
い-adjたかいたかくない
な-adjしずかだしずかじゃない
noun学生だ学生じゃない
ない-form rule (う-verbs)Change final -u → -a, then add ない. のむ → のま + ない. Exception: ある → ない (not あらない).
たべる？
Eating? / Want to eat?
いかない。
I'm not going.
2

Short forms in informal speech — How conversations actually sound

Plain forms are used between friends, family, peers, and to inferiors. Strangers, customers, and elders still get ます-form. Final particles (ね・よ・の・かな) do extra work in casual speech.

〈polite〉あした 何を しますか。
What will you do tomorrow?
〈casual〉あした 何 する？
Whatcha doing tomorrow?
Casual questions drop the か and just raise intonation. たべる？ with a rising tone = "Eating?"
3

～と思います — I think that ___

Use the plain form of the embedded clause, then と思います.

Pattern[plain form clause] と 思います
メアリーさんは あした 来ると思います。
I think Mary will come tomorrow.
この本は おもしろくないと思います。
I don't think this book is interesting.
Never use ます-form before と思います: ×行きますと思います. Always plain.
4

～と言っていました — (Someone) said that ___

Quoting indirect speech. Plain form + と言っていました.

たけしさんは あした 来ないと言っていました。
Takeshi said he won't come tomorrow.
If you want to quote literally (their exact words), wrap the quote in 「」: 「来ない」と言いました.
5

～ないでください — Please don't ___

Opposite of ～てください. Use the ない-form + でください.

ここで しゃしんを とらないでください。
Please don't take pictures here.
わすれないでください。
Please don't forget.
6

Modifying nouns with verbs/adjectives — Relative clauses in disguise

To attach a verb-phrase or adjective-clause to a noun, put it before the noun in plain form. No "who/that/which" — Japanese just stacks the descriptor in front.

Pattern[plain clause] [noun] → "the [noun] that [clause]"
これは わたしが すんでいる うちです。
This is the house I live in.
きのう 食べた すしは おいしかった。
The sushi I ate yesterday was tasty.
せが 高い 人
a tall person ("a person whose height is tall")
Inside the modifier clause, the subject is marked with が, not は: ○わたしが食べたすし, ×わたしは食べたすし.
7

まだ ～ていません — Haven't yet ___

The right way to say "haven't done X yet": まだ + て-form + いません.

もう ひるごはんを 食べましたか。
Have you eaten lunch yet?
いいえ、まだ 食べていません。
No, I haven't eaten yet.
Lesson 9

Past Plain Forms & から

Past short forms and using から to give reasons.
1

Past short forms — verbs — Plain past tense

Plain past for verbs = the た-form. It follows the exact same conjugation as the て-form — just swap て→た and で→だ.

Dict. formて-formた-form
たべるたべてたべた
のむのんでのんだ
かくかいてかいた
いくいっていった
するしてした
くるきてきた
きのう 京都に 行った。
I went to Kyoto yesterday.
もう しゅくだいを した。
I already did my homework.
2

Past short forms — negatives, adjectives, nouns — Patterns to memorize

Most past-negative plain forms are built from the ない-form (which conjugates like an い-adjective).

Past affirmativePast negative
verb食べた食べなかった
い-adjたかかったたかくなかった
な-adjしずかだったしずかじゃなかった
noun学生だった学生じゃなかった
Notice: ない conjugates like an い-adjective. 食べない → 食べなかった follows the same logic as たかい → たかかった.
3

Past short forms in quoted speech — Reporting what happened

Same as L8's quoting patterns, just with past short forms.

メアリーさんは きのう パーティーに 行ったと言っていました。
Mary said she went to the party yesterday.
この本は おもしろかったと思います。
I think this book was interesting.
4

Modifying nouns with past clauses — The ___ that I did

Stack the past plain clause in front of the noun. Same logic as L8 — Japanese just packs the modifier in front, no relative pronouns.

きのう 食べた すしは おいしかった。
The sushi I ate yesterday was good.
メアリーさんが 書いた メールは ながかった。
The email Mary wrote was long.
5

～から (revisited) — giving reasons — Now with plain forms

Same から as L6, but now you can use plain forms in front of it — common in casual speech, and required in some patterns.

あした テストが あるから、 今晩 べんきょうする。
I'll study tonight because there's a test tomorrow.
さむかったから、 出かけませんでした。
Because it was cold, I didn't go out.
In polite contexts, you can also keep ます-form before から: テストがありますから、勉強します. Both are fine; plain-form-before-から is more relaxed.
Lesson 10

Comparisons & Nominalization

Comparing things (2 items, 3+ items), nominalizing verbs with の, the difference between つもり/たい.
1

Comparison between two items — X is more ___ than Y

Three patterns, same meaning. The most flexible is XのほうがYより [adj] です.

PatternsX のほうが Y より [adj] です  |  X は Y より [adj] です
メアリーさんのほうが たけしさんより せが 高いです。
Mary is taller than Takeshi.
車は バイクより 高いです。
Cars are more expensive than motorcycles.
To ask a comparison: X と Y と、どちらのほうが [adj] ですか — "Between X and Y, which is more [adj]?"
2

Comparison among 3+ items — X is the most ___ of all

Use いちばん ("number one") in front of the adjective. The group of comparison is marked with の中で.

Pattern[group] の中で X が いちばん [adj] です
くだものの中で りんごが いちばん 好きです。
Of all fruits, I like apples the most.
クラスの中で たけしさんが いちばん せが 高いです。
Of the class, Takeshi is the tallest.
Question form: [group]の中で どれ/だれ/なに/どこ が いちばん [adj] ですか. The question word matches the category — どれ for things, だれ for people, etc.
3

Adjective + の — The ___ one

To say "the cheap one," "the new one," etc., put の after the adjective in place of a noun.

もっと 安いのを 見せてください。
Please show me a cheaper one.
赤いのが いいです。
I'll take the red one.
4

～つもりだ — I intend to ___

Use the dictionary form (plain non-past) + つもりです. This expresses a definite intention — something you've decided on.

Pattern[dict. form] つもりです
らいねん 日本へ 行くつもりです。
I intend to go to Japan next year.
あした はたらかないつもりです。
I intend not to work tomorrow.
つもり vs たい: ～たい (L11) is "want to" — a desire. ～つもり is "intend to" — a plan/decision. "I want to go" ≠ "I plan to go."
5

Adjective → adverb — Doing it ___ly

To turn an adjective into an adverb (modifying a verb), follow these conversion rules.

TypeRuleExample
い-adjdrop い, add くはやい → はやく
な-adjadd にしずか → しずかに
はやく 起きてください。
Please wake up early.
しずかに してください。
Please be quiet.
いい → よく, not ×いく.
6

～なる — Becoming ___

"To become." Combine with the adverb form of an adjective (or a noun + に).

い-adjさむく なるget cold
な-adjきれいに なるbecome pretty
noun先生に なるbecome a teacher
さむくなりました。
It's gotten cold.
日本語が じょうずに なりたいです。
I want to get good at Japanese.
Lesson 11

～たい, ～たり, Experience

Wanting to do things, listing example actions, talking about past experiences, and counting per unit.
1

～たい — I want to ___

Want to do something. Drop ます from the verb stem and add たい. The result conjugates like an い-adjective.

Pattern[verb stem] + たい(です)
AffirmativeNegative
Non-past食べたい(です)食べたくない(です)
Past食べたかった(です)食べたくなかった(です)
日本へ 行きたいです。
I want to go to Japan.
きのう 何も したくなかった。
I didn't want to do anything yesterday.
～たい only describes the speaker's own desires (or asks about the listener's). For third-person desires, use ～たがっている (covered later).
Object particle change: を often becomes が when using ～たい. 水を飲む → 水が飲みたい is very common (both are acceptable).
2

～たことがある — I have ___ed (experience)

Past plain form (た-form) + ことがある = "I have had the experience of ___ing."

Pattern[た-form] + ことがあります／ありません
日本へ 行ったことが あります。
I have been to Japan.
すしを 食べたことが ありません。
I have never eaten sushi.
Don't use this for things you did recently or specifically — ○きのうすしを食べました, not ×きのうすしを食べたことがあります. It's about life experience, not specific events.
3

～たり、～たり する — Doing things like ___ and ___

List a couple of example activities — implying "among other things." Both verbs in た-form, each followed by り, finished with する.

Pattern[たり] [たり] する → tense is set by する
しゅうまつは えいがを みたり、 本を よんだり します。
On weekends I do things like watching movies and reading books.
きのうは そうじを したり、 せんたくを したりしました。
Yesterday I cleaned, did laundry, and so on.
This is not just "X and Y." It implies "X, Y, and other similar things." For a complete "X and Y (and that's all)," use the て-form instead.
4

[freq] に [count] — X times per Y

Frequency over a period. The period takes に, the count comes after.

Pattern[period] に [count] times
一週間に 三回 ジムへ 行きます。
I go to the gym 3 times a week.
一日に 八時間 ねます。
I sleep 8 hours a day.
Lesson 12

～んです, ～すぎる, ～ましょうか, でしょう

Giving and seeking explanations, excess, offering help, and softer predictions.
1

～んです — Giving / asking for an explanation

Attach んです to a plain form to signal "there's a reason / let me explain" or "what's going on?" Massively common in real conversation.

Attaching んですverb (plain) + んです | い-adj + んです | な-adj/noun + な + んです
どうして 学校を 休んだんですか。
How come you skipped school? (asking for explanation)
ねつが あるんです。
I have a fever. (here's why)
この まちは しずかなんです。
(You see,) this town is quiet.
Use ～な (not だ) before んです with な-adjectives and nouns: ○しずかなんです, ×しずかだんです. With verbs and い-adjectives, no extra particle: 行くんです, たかいんです.
In casual speech, んです often becomes の (especially among women) or んだ. どうして来なかったの？
2

～すぎる — Too ___

Tack すぎる onto a stem to mean "excessively." すぎる itself is a る-verb — it conjugates like one.

TypeStem+ すぎる
verb食べ-食べすぎる
い-adj (drop い)たか-たかすぎる
な-adj (just stem)しずかしずかすぎる
ゆうべ 食べすぎました。
I ate too much last night.
この くつは 大きすぎます。
These shoes are too big.
3

～ほうがいいです — You'd better ___ / It's better to ___

Suggesting/recommending. Use the past plain form (た-form) for advice; use the negative plain (ない-form) for "better not to."

Affirmative[た-form] ほうがいいです → "You'd better ___"
Negative[ない-form] ほうがいいです → "You'd better not ___"
くすりを 飲んだほうが いいですよ。
You should take medicine.
たばこを すわないほうが いいです。
You'd better not smoke.
Note the form mix: positive advice uses past, negative advice uses non-past negative. Yes, it's weird. Memorize it.
4

～ので — Because ___ (softer than から)

Same meaning as から, but feels softer and more polite. Often used to give reasons for asking favors or making excuses.

Pattern[plain form] ので、… (な-adj/noun + なので)
じかんが ないので、 行けません。
I can't go because I don't have time.
べんりなので、 よく つかいます。
Because it's convenient, I use it often.
5

～ましょうか — Shall I ___ ?

L5 introduced ～ましょうか for "shall we?" — but the same form is also used to offer to do something for someone: "Shall I ___?"

にもつを もちましょうか。
Shall I carry your luggage?
まどを 開けましょうか。
Shall I open the window?
6

でしょう — I bet / probably (softer than だ)

Replaces です at the end of a sentence to make a guess or seek agreement. Tone matters — rising = "right?" / falling = "probably."

あした、雨が ふるでしょう。
It'll probably rain tomorrow.
たぶん あしたは さむくないでしょう。
I bet it won't be cold tomorrow.
これ、おいしいでしょう？
This is tasty, right? (rising — seeking agreement)
Attach でしょう directly to plain forms — but don't use だ in front: ○野菜でしょう, ×野菜だでしょう.

Obligation & Permission

Saying you must / mustn't / don't have to do something. Built from L6's te-form and L8's ない-form by stacking them with いけない/だめ/ならない.
1

～なければいけません — You must do ___

The standard "must" pattern. It's built by double negation: take the ない-form, drop the い, add ければいけません. Literally "if you don't do X, it won't do" → "you must do X."

Recipe[ない-form] − い + ければいけません → 行かない → 行かなければいけません
あした 早く 起きなければいけません。
I have to get up early tomorrow.
くすりを 飲まなければいけません。
You must take the medicine.
しゅくだいを しなければいけません。
I have to do my homework.
Mnemonically: this is just L6's ～てはいけません ("doing X is no good") flipped to the negative side: ～なくては いけません ("not doing X is no good") = "you must do it." The ～なければ form is a slightly older conditional twin of ～なくては — same meaning.
2

～なくてはいけません — You must do ___ (variant)

Exact same meaning as ～なければいけません, just a different conditional construction underneath. Use whichever rolls off your tongue.

Recipe[ない-form] − い + くてはいけません → 食べない → 食べなくてはいけません
六時までに 帰らなくてはいけません。
I have to be back by six.
毎日 れんしゅうしなくてはいけません。
I have to practice every day.
In writing and formal speech you'll also see ～なくてはなりません — even more formal, same meaning. なる here is closer to "work out / be acceptable" than "become."
3

～ないといけません — You must do ___ (another variant)

Yet another way to say "must." Uses the conditional particle と instead. Slightly more conversational than the others, but still polite. All three (～なければ / ～なくては / ～ないと) mean the same thing.

Recipe[ない-form] + と + いけません → 行かない → 行かないといけません
そろそろ 行かないといけません。
I should be heading off soon.
先生に 聞かないといけません。
I'll have to ask the teacher.
4

～なきゃ / ～なくちゃ — Gotta do ___ (very casual)

The contraction you actually hear in speech. なければ shrinks to なきゃ; なくては shrinks to なくちゃ. The trailing いけない usually gets dropped entirely — implied from context.

Shortcuts～なければ(いけない) → ～なきゃ  |  ～なくては(いけない) → ～なくちゃ
もう 行かなきゃ。
Gotta go.
勉強しなくちゃ。
Gotta study.
これ、覚えなきゃね。
Gotta memorize this, huh.
This is by far the most common form in anime, NHK Easy News interviews, podcasts, etc. If you only learn one of the obligation forms for listening comprehension, learn this one.
5

～なくてもいいです — You don't have to do ___

The opposite of "must do." Built from the ない-form's て-form (think of なくて as the "te-form of ない") + も + いいです. Same pattern shape as L6's ～てもいいです (permission), but applied to a negative.

Recipe[ない-form] − い + くてもいいです → 来ない → 来なくてもいいです
あした 来なくてもいいですよ。
You don't have to come tomorrow.
お金を 払わなくてもいいです。
You don't have to pay.
Watch the distinction. ～なくてもいい = "don't have to" (it's optional). ～てはいけない = "must not" (it's forbidden). English "don't ___" can mean either — Japanese forces you to pick.
6

～ちゃいけない / ～じゃいけない — Must not (casual contractions)

L6 taught ～てはいけません ("must not do"). In speech, ～ては contracts to ～ちゃ, and ～では contracts to ～じゃ. The casual prohibition you'll hear constantly.

Shortcuts～ては → ～ちゃ  |  ～では → ～じゃ
ここに 入っちゃいけない。
You can't come in here.
これ、飲んじゃいけないよ。
You're not allowed to drink this.
そんなこと 言っちゃだめ！
Don't say things like that!
だめ ("no good") often replaces いけない in casual speech — even more conversational. 食べちゃだめ = "don't eat that."
7

動詞 + な — Don't ___ (blunt prohibition)

Attach な directly to a verb's dictionary form for a sharp, blunt "don't!" Used by men, in commands, between close friends, or by angry people. Be careful — it's strong.

Pattern[dictionary form] + な → 行くな / 見るな / するな
行くな！
Don't go!
見るな。
Don't look.
そんなこと 言うな。
Don't say that.
Don't confuse this prohibitive な with the sentence-ending な for reflection (covered in Section B) or the な-adjective connector. Context — and the bluntness — usually makes it clear.
8

～ないで(ください) — Without doing / please don't

Two related uses. (1) With ください: a polite "please don't" (already in L8). (2) Without ください: "do B without doing A."

Use 1 — request[ない-form] + でください → 心配しない → 心配しないでください
Use 2 — without doing[ない-form] + で、[main clause]
心配しないでください。
Please don't worry.
じしょを 使わないで、 書いてみてください。
Try writing it without using a dictionary.
朝ごはんを 食べないで、 学校へ 行きました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.
Bridge to N5 · Section B

Casual Speech Building Blocks

The casual-register pieces Genki teaches stiffly or skips. With these you can read manga, follow anime dialogue, and chat with friends naturally.
1

だ — Plain copula — casual です

だ is the plain (non-polite) form of です. You drop it in casual speech with friends, use it in inner monologue and writing, and it sits at the root of dozens of grammar patterns (だから, だろう, だけど...).

PolitePlain
Non-past学生です学生だ
Past学生でした学生だった
Non-past neg.学生じゃないです学生じゃない
Past neg.学生じゃなかったです学生じゃなかった
これ、おれの本だ。
This is my book.
きのうは 雨だった。
It was raining yesterday.
In questions, casual speech drops だ entirely. 学生？ with rising tone, not ×学生だ？. The だ only appears in statements.
Female speakers often drop だ in declarations too: これ、私の本. Male speakers tend to keep it. Generalization, not a rule.
2

だから — Because / so

Reason-marker that joins two sentences with "so" / "therefore". Comes between the reason and the result (unlike L6's から, which sits at the end of the reason clause). Casual register; polite version is ですから.

Pattern[reason]。だから、[result]。
あした テストだ。だから、 今晩 勉強する。
Tomorrow's a test. So I'm studying tonight.
頭が いたい。だから、 早く 寝るよ。
My head hurts. So I'm going to bed early.
Compare to L6's から: あしたテストがあるから、勉強する packs both clauses into one sentence. だから splits them — useful when the reason needs to stand on its own.
3

だろう — Probably / right? (casual でしょう)

Plain form of L12's でしょう. Same two uses: (1) softer "probably," (2) rising-tone "right?" for seeking agreement. Used in casual speech and inner monologue.

あした 雨だろう。
It'll probably rain tomorrow.
これ、お前のだろう？
This is yours, right?
Male speakers favor だろう; female speakers more often soften to でしょう even in casual speech. In anime, you'll hear blunt male characters say だろう a lot.
4

けど / けれども — But / however (casual but-words)

Three flavors of "but," same meaning, different register. けど is most casual; けれど is in the middle; けれども is most formal. All attach to plain or polite forms.

WordRegister
けどMost casual — speech with friends
けれどNeutral
けれどもFormal, written
がFormal, written (same job)
行きたいけど、 時間が ない。
I want to go, but I don't have time.
おいしいけれども、 高いです。
It's tasty, but expensive.
けど can also soften a statement when it's at the end of a sentence, with no following clause: ちょっと聞きたいんですけど… ("I wanted to ask you something, but…"). The hanging "but" is an invitation to engage.
5

んです / のです — Explanation marker (revisited)

L12 covered んです. のです is the same pattern, just slightly more formal and more common in writing. Both signal "there's a reason" / "let me explain" / "what's going on?"

Same attachment rulesverb (plain) + んです/のです | い-adj + んです/のです | な-adj/noun + な + んです/のです
どうして 来なかったのですか。
How come you didn't come? (formal — written/官式)
どうして 来なかったんですか。
Same meaning, more conversational.
In casual speech, んです often becomes just の — especially in questions. 来なかったの？ = "You didn't come? (what's up?)". Very common pattern.
6

～なあ — Sentence-ending reflection

A reflective sentence-ender expressing the speaker's feeling — admiration, longing, mild surprise, sigh. Often translated as "…huh" or "…I wish…" Pure emotion-tagging, no grammatical role.

きれいだなあ。
Wow, that's beautiful.
いいなあ、その時計。
Nice watch (I want one).
帰りたいなあ。
Man, I wanna go home.
Often written as ～な alone in casual writing — the elongation is implied. Don't confuse with the prohibitive ～な from Section A: prohibitive attaches to dictionary form verbs (行くな); reflective attaches to plain forms and feels emotional.
7

～よ — Sentence-ending information marker

L1 introduced this briefly. Here's the fuller picture: よ signals "I'm telling you something you don't already know" — sharing info, asserting, sometimes mildly correcting. Pairs well with ね (which seeks agreement on shared knowledge).

これ、おいしいよ。
This is good (FYI).
もう おそいよ。
It's getting late, you know.
あ、そこ あぶないですよ。
Hey — that spot's dangerous.
Stacking: ～よね is common — "I'm telling you, right?" — asserting something and inviting agreement. おいしいよね = "this is good, isn't it?"
Bridge to N5 · Section C

Connectors & Question Words

The glue between sentences and the question words for digging deeper. With these you stop writing in isolated sentences and start writing connected discourse.
1

でも — But / however

Sentence-initial "but." Comes at the start of the second sentence, after a full stop. More casual than しかし.

Pattern[sentence 1]。でも、[sentence 2]。
行きたかった。でも、 時間が なかった。
I wanted to go. But I didn't have time.
コーヒーが 好きです。でも、 きょうは 紅茶に します。
I like coffee. But today I'll go with tea.
Don't put でも mid-sentence between clauses — that's けど's job. でも always starts a new sentence.
2

しかし — But / however (formal)

Formal/written "but." Same position as でも (sentence-initial), same meaning, more bookish. You'll see it in news articles and essays; you'd rarely say it out loud in casual conversation.

この案には メリットが ある。しかし、 リスクも 大きい。
This plan has merit. However, the risks are also large.
Register ladder for "but": けど (casual) → けれど → でも → けれども → しかし → が (most formal).
3

それから — And then / after that

Sequencing connector — "after that, …" Use it to chain events in order. Also useful for adding items to a list ("…and another thing…").

朝、おきました。それから、 朝ごはんを 食べました。
I woke up in the morning. Then I ate breakfast.
コーヒー、おねがいします。それから、サラダも。
Coffee, please. And a salad too.
4

そして — And / and so

A more general additive connector. Closer to written-English "and" linking sentences. Less time-sequential than それから; more about adding a related idea.

メアリーさんは あたまが いいです。そして、 やさしいです。
Mary is smart. And she's kind.
それから implies sequence ("then…"). そして just adds ("and also…"). Often interchangeable but the nuance differs.
5

どんな — What kind of ___

Question word that attaches to a noun: "what kind of ___?" Functions like a な-adjective grammatically.

Patternどんな + [noun] + ですか
どんな 音楽が 好きですか。
What kind of music do you like?
どんな 人ですか。
What kind of person is he/she?
Don't confuse どんな with なに. なにが好きですか = "what do you like?" (asks for a specific thing). どんなのが好きですか = "what kind do you like?" (asks for a category).
6

どうして / なぜ — Why

Two ways to ask "why." どうして is most common in speech; なぜ is slightly more formal/written. Often paired with んです to soften.

どうして 来なかったんですか。
Why didn't you come?
なぜ そう 思いますか。
Why do you think so?
Even more casual: なんで. なんで遅れたの？ = "why are you late?" Casual-only — don't use it with your boss.
7

どうやって — How / by what means

Asks for a method. Different from どう alone ("how was it?" — asking impression). どうやって specifically wants instructions or a procedure.

駅まで どうやって 行きますか。
How do I get to the station?
これ、どうやって 食べるの？
How do I eat this?
8

か〜か — X or Y

Use か between two (or more) nouns to mean "X or Y" — choosing among alternatives. Note: the second か can sometimes be dropped.

Pattern[noun]か [noun]か
コーヒーか 紅茶か、 どちらが いいですか。
Which is better, coffee or tea?
ペンか えんぴつで 書いてください。
Please write with a pen or pencil.
9

や — And / or (non-exhaustive listing)

Lists two or more nouns as examples, implying "…and other things too." Different from と (which lists exhaustively: "X and Y, that's all").

CompareXとY = X and Y (only these) | XやY = X and Y (among others)
つくえの上に 本や ペンが あります。
There are books, pens, and other things on the desk.
週末は 映画を 見たり、本を 読んだりします。
(For comparison — verb listing uses ～たり instead.)
Often paired with ～など at the end for explicit "…etc.": りんごやみかんなど = "apples, oranges, and so on."
10

とき — When ___

Attach a plain-form clause + とき to mean "when [clause]." Acts like a time-expression noun.

Pattern[plain-form clause] + とき(に)、[main clause]
子どもの とき、 よく 川で あそびました。
When I was a kid, I often played in the river.
日本へ 行くとき、 おみやげを 買います。
When I go to Japan, I'll buy souvenirs. (before going — non-past)
日本へ 行ったとき、 おみやげを 買いました。
When I went to Japan, I bought souvenirs. (after arriving — past)
Tense matters. 行くとき (non-past) implies "before/while going." 行ったとき (past) implies "when I had arrived." Same event, different stage. Genki II covers this in depth — for now, just notice the tense.
Bridge to N5 · Section D

Useful Patterns

The remaining N5 odds and ends — high-frequency patterns that don't fit a single topic but show up constantly.
1

だけ — Only / just

Limits the noun or quantity it follows. Comes after the thing it limits.

Pattern[noun/quantity] + だけ
水だけ 飲みます。
I'll just drink water.
一つだけ ください。
Just one, please.
あなただけ。
Only you.
2

くらい / ぐらい — About / approximately

Vague-quantifier. Attaches to a number or time to mean "about." くらい and ぐらい are interchangeable.

Pattern[quantity/time] + くらい／ぐらい
一時間ぐらい かかります。
It takes about an hour.
三人くらい 来ました。
About three people came.
Different from ～ごろ, which means "around a point in time": 三時ごろ = "around 3 o'clock." くらい is for amounts/durations.
3

～方 (かた) — How to do ___ / way of doing

Attach 方 to the verb stem (ます-form minus ます) to make a noun meaning "the way of ___ing."

Pattern[verb stem] + 方 → 使う → 使い方
使い方を 教えてください。
Please teach me how to use it.
食べ方が わかりません。
I don't know how to eat this.
漢字の 書き方
the way to write kanji
4

にする — Decide on ___ / I'll go with ___

Used when picking from options — ordering at a restaurant, choosing a color, settling a decision. The thing chosen is marked with に.

Pattern[choice] + にします
コーヒーに します。
I'll have coffee. (ordering)
どれに しますか。
Which one will you go with?
赤に しました。
I went with the red one.
5

～をください — Please give me ___

Polite request for an item. Different from ～てください (which requests an action).

Pattern[noun] + を + ください
水を ください。
Water, please.
メニューを ください。
Could I have the menu?
～をください asks for a thing. ～てください asks for an action. Different をvs.て.
6

～のが上手／下手／好き — Good at / bad at / like doing

Verb nominalizer の turns a verb into a noun, which then takes the L5 "liking" pattern. Pattern: [plain verb] + のが + 上手／下手／好き.

Recipe[dict. form] + のが + [上手／下手／好き]です
ピアノを 弾くのが 上手です。
I'm good at playing piano.
人前で 話すのが 下手です。
I'm bad at speaking in public.
本を 読むのが 好きです。
I like reading books.
Notice the が! Just like L5's 好きです, the thing-liked/done takes が, not を.
7

verb + の — Verb nominalizer

Add の to the dictionary form of a verb to turn it into a noun. Use when you want to talk about "the act of ___ing" as a subject or object.

Pattern[dict. form] + の → 食べる → 食べるの ("eating" / "to eat")
料理するのが 好きです。
I like cooking.
勉強するのは むずかしい。
Studying is hard.
Compare to L1's possessive の: たけしさんの本 ("Takeshi's book"). Same particle, different job — possession vs nominalization. Context tells you which.
8

～てある — Has been done (resulting state)

Describes a resulting state from a deliberate prior action — someone did X, and now it stays done. Different from L7's ～ている for resulting state (intransitive verbs); ～てある uses transitive verbs and emphasizes that someone did it on purpose.

Pattern[transitive te-form] + ある
まどが 開けてあります。
The window has been left open. (someone opened it deliberately)
ホテルは 予約してあります。
The hotel has been booked.
Compare carefully: まどが開いている (intransitive 開く) = "the window is open" (just the state). まどが開けてある (transitive 開ける) = "the window has been opened" (someone did it). Subtle but real difference.
9

お / ご — Honorific prefixes

Attach お or ご in front of a noun for politeness/respect. Roughly: お goes on words of Japanese origin (和語); ご goes on words of Chinese origin (漢語). With many high-frequency words, you'll see them so often they essentially become part of the word.

BaseWith prefix
名前 (name)お名前
金 (money)お金
すしおすし
家族 (family)ご家族
主人 (husband)ご主人
連絡 (contact)ご連絡
There are exceptions and habit-only attachments (e.g. お茶, お風呂, お弁当 are essentially always with お). Don't try to derive the rule from scratch — just notice the patterns as you encounter them.
10

がほしい — Want ___ (a noun)

Expresses wanting a thing. The thing wanted is marked with が (like L5's 好き). Different from L11's ～たい, which expresses wanting to do something.

Pattern[noun] + が + ほしい(です)
新しい パソコンが ほしいです。
I want a new computer.
時間が ほしい。
I want (more) time.
Same restriction as ～たい: がほしい describes only the speaker's own desires (or asks about the listener's). For third-person wants, use ～をほしがっている.
11

とても / すごく — Very

Intensifier before an adjective. とても is neutral/polite; すごく is more casual.

とても おもしろいです。
It's very interesting.
すごく いい！
Super good!
With negatives, switch to あまり: とてもおもしろくない sounds off; use あまりおもしろくない = "not very interesting." Same pattern as L3's frequency adverbs.
12

いつも / よく / ときどき — Always / often / sometimes (review)

Frequency adverbs that go right before the verb. L3 covered よく, ときどき, あまり, ぜんぜん. Add いつも (always) to round out the set.

いつも 朝ごはんを 食べます。
I always eat breakfast.
いつも ありがとう。
Thanks, as always.
13

一緒に — Together

"Together" adverb — modifies a verb, often paired with ～と (with) to specify who you're together with.

いっしょに 行きましょう。
Let's go together.
ともだちと いっしょに 勉強しました。
I studied together with a friend.
